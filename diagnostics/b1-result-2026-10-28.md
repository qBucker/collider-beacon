## Findings

### F1 — the storage rule is round-to-nearest mantissa

For each matched jet the PFNano value is compared with four candidate
transformations of the MiniAOD float32 value. On 102,977 jets:

| branch | rule | exact share |
|---|---|---|
| Jet_pt | mantissa round to 10 explicit bits | 99.8534% |
| Jet_mass | mantissa round to 10 explicit bits | 99.8738% |
| Jet_eta | mantissa round to 12 explicit bits | 99.9922% |
| Jet_phi | mantissa round to 12 explicit bits | 99.9796% |

The next-best rules are far behind (fixed-point grids 60-87%, truncation
50%). The storage rule is therefore a **relative-precision rounding**, not a
fixed-point grid and not a truncation. This supersedes the "truncation"
phrasing used for the angular branches: a round-to-nearest value still carries
at least 11 (12-bit) or 13 (10-bit) trailing zeros, and the carry cases
produce the octave-dependent trailing-zero tail measured in C5/B5.

### F2 — the PFNano jet collection carries a pT > 15 GeV selection

The per-event jet counts agree exactly once the same selection is applied to
the MiniAOD collection (116,103/116,103). Without it, 82% of the matched
events have different counts. This is a product-level selection, not a
storage effect.

### F3 — the residual tail is one quantum

The non-reproducing jets differ by exactly one quantum of the selected rule:

- Jet_pt: residuals -0.015625 or +-0.0078125 GeV (the 10-bit quantum at 32 and
  16 GeV); 22 of 6,166 jets in a 20,000-entry subset (0.36%); 0.15% over the
  full sample.
- Jet_mass: residuals ~1.95e-3 GeV, one quantum at ~2 GeV; 4 of 6,166
  (0.065%).

The one-quantum flips are consistent with a second rounding step in the
producer (the polar components derived from already-rounded quantities),
not with a different storage rule. The producer source is not available in
the file, so this attribution is inferred, not demonstrated.
